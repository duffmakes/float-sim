7 · Sea state (waves)

Heading β

β = direction the waves travel, relative to the model's +X axis: 180° head seas, 90° beam seas, 0° following.

On: the body is carried by the Stokes drift current and pushed by waves it reflects (Ursell barrier reflection, Longuet-Higgins drift force), against drag on its speed relative to the current (Cd ≈ 1 for a bluff hull, Hoerner). Off: a very soft horizontal spring keeps it on station; wave-frequency surge and sway are unchanged.

Wave & hydrodynamic settings

Spreading 0 gives long-crested waves; s > 0 applies cos2s short-crested spreading.

On: the wave runs and the drop test use added mass and radiation damping from the panel method (section 8), taken at each mode's natural frequency (surge, sway and yaw at the wave frequency). They come from the last panel-method RAO sweep of this body, or from a quick coarse sweep the first time (a few seconds). The A fields below then apply to the estimates only. Off: the estimates below.

Extra (viscous) damping, as a fraction of critical, added to the panel-method damping in the RAOs and the simulations. Potential flow has very little roll damping, so real roll peaks are lower; 0.02–0.05 is typical for a bare hull.

Estimates (used without the panel method): strip-theory semicircle for heave, 0.2·I for roll, damping as a fraction of critical.

Long-wave (G.I. Taylor) correction: adds added mass × the incident acceleration averaged over the submerged volume. In the trade study (docs/rao-trade-study.md) it fixes the 50 % low horizontal force of Froude–Krylov (spar within 5 % of MacCamy–Fuchs). For heave it helps in long waves but overcorrects near resonance unless A33 is calibrated, so it is off by default.

8 · RAO sweep

Panel method: solves the linear diffraction–radiation problem on the wetted hull (deep water, zero speed, as WAMIT's low-order method) for frequency-dependent added mass, radiation damping and wave exciting forces with all couplings, then the 6-DOF RAOs. Checked against WAMIT, Capytaine and the Kramer sphere decay test: within 2 % for surge, sway and heave and 5 % for rotations on buoys (docs/bem-validation.md). Panel counts are for flat-sided hulls; curved hulls get up to about 3× more, as panels are kept below 1/20 of the circumference where the hull curves. Runs in background workers; Standard takes from a few seconds to a few minutes. Extra viscous damping: section 7 → Wave & hydrodynamic settings.

Locked: the body is placed for a drop / rise test. Use Reset position in section 9 to compute RAOs.

9 · Drop / rise test

Release the body from a chosen height to watch it fall into the water, plunge and recover, or rise from below the surface and settle. Uses the mass, CoG, inertia and orientation above. It does not affect the RAO or sea-state tools, which are locked while the body is placed away from its float position.

10 · Tracked points

Points on the body to follow during the wave runs: an antenna, GPS or solar panel (time above water, underwater stretches) or an IMU (accelerations). Coordinates in the model's own axes, in the units chosen in section 1. A clearance counts the point as underwater until it is that far above the surface (e.g. an antenna that needs to stand clear).

11 · Viscous drag & heave plates

Quadratic drag ½ρCdA|u|u on the body's speed relative to the wave orbital flow, along the floating body's own axes; A is the submerged area projected along each axis (X and Y: side areas, Z: the area seen from below). Rotations get drag from the same coefficients on the wetted panels. The RAOs and the sea-state table use the equivalent linear damping for the sea state in section 7 (stochastic linearisation), so they include it too. Wave runs use the full quadratic drag.

Heave plates

Thin discs fixed to the body, horizontal when floating; centre in model coordinates. Each adds added mass ρD³/3 normal to the plate, an inertia force from the wave acceleration and drag ½ρCd(πD²/4)|u|u. Cd ≈ 1.2 in steady flow (Hoerner); oscillating plates at small motion amplitudes often show higher values, so use test data if you have it. Leave the plate out of the CAD model (a thin disc adds almost nothing there), but include its mass in section 2.

12 · Mooring

Anchors on the seabed at the anchor radius from the fairlead's position above them at the start, spread evenly in direction (measured like the wave heading, from the model's +X). The body then settles where the lines (and the steady wind) balance: a single line pulls it toward its anchor until the line goes slack, so the wave runs start there. Lines are in metres; the fairlead is in model coordinates.

Line properties

Presets are typical values (chain: DNV-OS-E301 / Orcina, mass 19.9D² t/m and EA 0.854·10⁸D² kN, volume-equivalent diameter 1.8D). Check the manufacturer's data for ropes, whose stiffness depends on load and age. The diameter sets drag and added mass; buoyancy comes from the material density.

Quasi-static: elastic catenary with seabed contact at every step (Jonkman 2007; no seabed friction). Dynamic: a lumped-mass line (MoorDyn-style, Hall & Goupee 2015) with tension-only segments, line drag in the wave flow and seabed contact, so it shows snap loads when a slack line goes taut. The RAOs use the catenary's linear stiffness at the mean position (under the steady wind, if on). The line's pull is included in the floating draft of the wave and RAO tools; the Hydrostatics tab shows the free-floating body.

13 · Wind

Wind profile U(z) = U10(z/10)0.12 over open sea, with gusts from the Frøya spectrum (DNV-RP-C205 §2.3). Every panel of the model above the instantaneous water surface takes ½ρairCdA|u|u on its projected area, with u the wind minus the panel's own velocity. Typical Cd: 1.0–1.2 for bluff shapes and masts, 1.28 for a flat panel face-on (Hoerner). Wave runs show the heel, drift (with wave drift on, or moored) and gust response. With wave drift off and no mooring, the mean push is held on station like the wave drift.

Hydrostatics: rigid body, uniform density for inertia. Waves: linear incident waves with Froude–Krylov pressure (Wheeler-stretched), added mass and damping from the panel method or estimates, zero forward speed, deep water (also for the wave kinematics on mooring lines; the water depth in section 12 only places the anchors).

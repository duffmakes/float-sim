The origin and axes of the reference csys from your CAD model, given in the file's coordinates (metres). Fixed axes: each rotation is about the file's X, Y or Z axis. Turning axes: each is about the axes as already turned. Axis directions need not be unit length; y completes a right-handed set. Check the axes listed below against your CAD.

About the reference csys

Everything Float Sim asks for or reports in body coordinates is in this csys: the CoG, the moments of inertia, tracked points, heave plates, the fairlead, the target waterline and the RAOs (motions at its origin, wave headings from its +x). Its +z should point up and +x forward when the body floats upright; otherwise turn the body with Start attitude. Numbers you have typed stay as typed when you change the csys, so they are read in the new one. The CoG you have not typed (the centroid default) stays where it is on the body.

Wave direction

Direction the waves travel, 0–360° from global +X (90° = toward +Y).

Drift
About drift

On: the body is carried by the Stokes drift current and pushed by waves it reflects (Ursell barrier reflection, Longuet-Higgins drift force), against drag on its speed relative to the current (Cd ≈ 1 for a bluff hull, Hoerner). Off: a very soft horizontal spring keeps it on station; wave-frequency surge and sway are unchanged.

Hydrodynamics & wave detailpanel-method A, B
About panel-method coefficients

On: the wave runs and the drop test use added mass and radiation damping from the panel method, taken at each mode's natural frequency (surge, sway and yaw at the wave frequency). They come from the last panel-method RAO sweep of this body, or from a quick coarse sweep the first time (a few seconds). The A fields below then apply to the estimates only. Off: the estimates below.

Extra (viscous) damping as a fraction of critical, added to the panel-method damping in the RAOs and simulations. Potential flow has very little roll damping; 0.02–0.05 is typical for a bare hull.

Estimates (used without the panel method)
About the diffraction correction

Adds added mass × the incident acceleration averaged over the submerged volume. In the trade study (docs/rao-trade-study.md) it fixes the 50 % low horizontal force of Froude–Krylov (spar within 5 % of MacCamy–Fuchs). For heave it helps in long waves but overcorrects near resonance unless A33 is calibrated, so it is off by default. Estimates: strip-theory semicircle for heave, 0.2·I for roll, damping as a fraction of critical.

Tracked points
About tracked points

Points on the body to follow during the wave runs: an antenna, GPS or solar panel (time above water, underwater stretches) or an IMU (accelerations). Ref csys, metres. A clearance counts the point as underwater until it is that far above the surface (e.g. an antenna that needs to stand clear).

Solver
About the panel method

Solves the linear diffraction–radiation problem on the wetted hull (deep water, zero speed, as WAMIT's low-order method) for frequency-dependent added mass, radiation damping and wave exciting forces with all couplings, then the 6-DOF RAOs. Checked against WAMIT, Capytaine and the Kramer sphere decay test: within 2 % for surge, sway and heave and 5 % for rotations on buoys. RAOs of the 12 test models match Capytaine within 4.3 % away from resonances: see the RAO solver review and Capytaine comparison. Panel counts are for flat-sided hulls; curved hulls get up to about 3× more. Runs in background workers; Standard takes from a few seconds to a few minutes. Extra viscous damping: Waves › Hydrodynamics.

About Solve with Capytaine

Solves the same wetted-surface panels, lid and symmetry with Capytaine (Ancellin & Dias 2019, direct formulation, deep water) on this computer, instead of Float Sim's own solver. Everything after the solve (RAOs, reference point, sea states, exports, simulators) is the same. It needs Python with Capytaine (pip install capytaine) and Float Sim started with ./serve.sh from that Python (or FLOATSIM_PYTHON=/path/to/python ./serve.sh). Capytaine runs one frequency per process, as many processes as the cores and free memory allow. Uses the Panels and Irregular-frequency settings of the panel method. How the two solvers compare: RAO solver review and Capytaine comparison.

/Mooring
Lines
Geometry
About the layout

Anchors on the seabed at the anchor radius from the fairlead's position above them at the start, spread evenly in direction. The body then settles where the lines (and the steady wind) balance: a single line pulls it toward its anchor until the line goes slack, so the wave runs start there.

Fairlead on the bodyref csys
Line model
About the line models

Quasi-static: elastic catenary with seabed contact at every step (Jonkman 2007; no seabed friction). Dynamic: a lumped-mass line (MoorDyn-style, Hall & Goupee 2015) with tension-only segments, line drag in the wave flow and seabed contact, so it shows snap loads when a slack line goes taut. The RAOs use the catenary's linear stiffness at the mean position. The line's pull is included in the floating draft of the wave and RAO tools; the Hydrostatics results show the free-floating body.

Line propertiesfrom preset

Presets are typical values (chain: DNV-OS-E301 / Orcina, mass 19.9D² t/m and EA 0.854·10⁸D² kN, volume-equivalent diameter 1.8D). Check the manufacturer's data for ropes. The diameter sets drag and added mass; buoyancy comes from the material density.

/Wind
Wind
About the wind model

Wind profile U(z) = U10(z/10)0.12 over open sea, with gusts from the Frøya spectrum (DNV-RP-C205 §2.3). Every panel of the model above the instantaneous water surface takes ½ρairCdA|u|u on its projected area, with u the wind minus the panel's own velocity. Typical Cd: 1.0–1.2 for bluff shapes and masts, 1.28 for a flat panel face-on (Hoerner). With wave drift off and no mooring, the mean push is held on station like the wave drift.

/Drag & heave plates
Hull drag (Morison)
About hull drag

Quadratic drag ½ρCdA|u|u on the body's speed relative to the wave orbital flow, along the floating body's own axes; A is the submerged area projected along each axis. Rotations get drag from the same coefficients on the wetted panels. The RAOs and the sea-state table use the equivalent linear damping for the sea state (stochastic linearisation); wave runs use the full quadratic drag.

Heave plates
About heave plates

Thin discs fixed to the body, horizontal when floating; centre in the ref csys. Each adds added mass ρD³/3 normal to the plate, an inertia force from the wave acceleration and drag ½ρCd(πD²/4)|u|u. Cd ≈ 1.2 in steady flow (Hoerner); oscillating plates at small motion amplitudes often show higher values. Leave the plate out of the CAD model, but include its mass in Float.

Method notes

Hydrostatics: rigid body, uniform density for the computed inertia. Units are metric throughout (m, kg, s, N).

Waves: linear incident waves with Froude–Krylov pressure (Wheeler-stretched), added mass and damping from the panel method or estimates, zero forward speed, deep water (also for the wave kinematics on mooring lines; the mooring water depth only places the anchors).

Wave direction in the Waves stage is measured from the global +X axis. RAO headings and the mooring line direction are measured from the reference csys +x axis.
